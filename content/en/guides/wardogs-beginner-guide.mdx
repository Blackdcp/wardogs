## Quick Answer

Treat the first $10,000 as seed money, not a disposable allowance. Buy a dependable primary weapon, ammunition, basic healing, and the minimum equipment needed for one useful role. Stay with the squad, learn the active Control Zone, earn through combat and support, and avoid staking most of the account on an advanced kit before you understand the match.

## Confirmed Facts

- WARDOGS places up to 100 players across three competing teams.
- The core match revolves around controlling a moving or randomized Control Zone.
- Cash persists and pays for the loadout taken into a life; death can therefore have an economic cost.
- Combat, revives, transport, supplies, and objective play can contribute to team progress and income.
- Weapons, armor, backpacks, medical tools, specialist launchers, and construction equipment can be tied to progression and build-specific prices.

## What Players Search For

New players most often ask what to buy, why their balance disappeared, how to spawn near combat, and whether they must select a fixed class. WARDOGS uses equipment and progression to shape a role rather than forcing every player into a rigid hero-style class. That freedom is useful, but it also makes an unfocused shopping cart expensive.

The first goal is not a perfect meta build. It is a repeatable kit that lets you reach the objective, survive ordinary contact, and perform one action the squad needs. Prices and unlock requirements observed in test footage can change, so use the decision order below even if a particular item moves.

## How to Use This Guide

Follow **First Purchase** before entering the map, **First Ten Minutes** once deployed, and **Recovering Cash** if several deaths leave the account under pressure. The advice prioritizes durable decisions over exact beta prices.

## First Purchase

Build from necessities outward. Take a primary weapon you can control, enough compatible ammunition for a normal engagement, and basic medical supplies. Add armor or storage only when it enables the intended role. A larger vest can expose more quick slots and improve handling convenience, while a backpack carries additional supplies, but neither replaces ammunition, healing, awareness, or teammates.

Do not buy every grenade, breaching tool, specialist launcher, premium backpack, and vehicle option at once. C4, mines, defibrillators, anti-air weapons, building hammers, and vehicle purchases can be powerful when the team has a plan; without that plan they turn starting cash into weight and risk. Leave money available for a second life.

## First Ten Minutes

Check the active objective and the distance before walking out of base. Use team transport when available and avoid taking a valuable vehicle alone simply because it is unlocked. On arrival, move with cover and maintain enough spacing that one explosion does not remove the whole group.

Inside the fight, value useful actions: hold or retake the Control Zone, spot threats, revive when the area is safe, deliver supplies, transport players, and protect builders or vehicle crews. WARDOGS is designed so support can matter economically as well as tactically. A quiet supply run can be more valuable than chasing one distant elimination.

## Death, Respawn and Cash

A purchased kit is at risk when the character dies. Do not assume the initial $10,000 refreshes every life. Before respawning, look at the remaining balance and the route back to combat. A cheap recovery loadout that can capture, revive, spot, or deliver supplies is better than repeatedly buying a kit the account can no longer sustain.

Use the main base and available team spawn vehicles correctly. A deployed FOB is a logistics position, not automatically a direct player spawn point in every observed build. Coordinate transport and protect mobile spawn assets instead of abandoning them near the enemy.

## Recovering Cash

When funds are low, simplify. Use the cheapest functional weapon path, carry only essential ammunition and healing, then choose actions with repeatable team value. Stay alive long enough for objective participation, revives, transport, spotting, and supply work to add up. Loot decisions and exact reward values can change during testing, so do not build a permanent money method around one beta number.

## FAQ

**Should I spend the entire first $10,000?**
Usually no. Keeping a reserve protects you from one early mistake and gives the second life a useful kit.

**Are there fixed classes?**
WARDOGS emphasizes flexible equipment-based roles. Progression tracks and tools still make specialization meaningful.

**Is support play worth doing?**
Yes. Official descriptions explicitly connect reviving, transport, logistics, and objective contribution with the wider team and cash loop.

**What should I learn before buying a vehicle?**
Learn the objective, crew seats, route, resupply needs, and safe return path. An expensive vehicle without a job can damage both the team and your balance.
