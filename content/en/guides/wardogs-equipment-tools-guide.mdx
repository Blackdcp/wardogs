## Quick Answer

Choose WARDOGS equipment from the squad task backward. Define whether you are building, supplying, repairing, treating, scouting, destroying armor or holding infantry space; then buy one useful tool, the storage and supplies it needs, and a weapon you can still operate. Tool price, capacity, charges, repair rate and unlocks are **build-sensitive** and are not confirmed as permanent live values.

## Confirmed Facts

Official pages confirm construction, logistics, vehicles, destruction, persistent cash and progression in the same battle. Dated gameplay shows role equipment and inventory tradeoffs. Final tool costs, capacities, repair rates, construction costs, charges and progression requirements have not been published as one stable live reference.

## What Players Search For

Players most often ask which tool to buy, how to build or repair, what a squad should carry, and whether specialist equipment is worth its inventory space. The answer depends on the task, route, target, protection, supplies and replacement plan. This guide organizes those decisions by workflow rather than by an unsupported tier list.

## Equipment is a workflow

A tool has no value when it cannot reach a target, lacks supplies, or sits with a player who does not know the squad plan. Official descriptions confirm construction, logistics, vehicles, destruction, cash and progression inside the same battle. That makes equipment a chain: purchase, carry, transport, protect, use, resupply and recover.

Before spawning, answer four questions. What result should this tool create? Where will it be used? Who protects the user? How is it replaced or resupplied? If the squad cannot answer those questions, a cheaper complete infantry kit is often more useful.

## Storage and protection first

Open the [gear catalogue](/en/items/gear) and compare packs, armor and helmets as parts of one loadout. Inventory space must fit the tool, ammunition, treatment and any carried resources. Protection can help the user reach the job, but the heaviest option may consume money or mobility needed for repeated trips.

Arrange important items consistently. Keep compatible magazines easy to identify and do not bury the role tool under loose ammunition. Recheck the current interface after patches because grid size, capacity, item footprint and interaction keys may change.

## Construction and FOB tools

Builders should travel with a site plan, security and the right resource flow. Confirm terrain, vehicle access, spawn exposure and the next Control Zone before placing a permanent-looking structure. Build the minimum useful network first: a supported position and logistics route are better than a decorative compound that consumes all supplies.

Use the [FOB guide](/en/guides/wardogs-fob-guide) for resource categories and placement workflow. Exact construction costs and tool charges can change, so read the live build menu. A builder should announce what is being placed and what supply is missing instead of silently exhausting the base.

## Repair and vehicle support

Repair equipment belongs close enough to help but far enough from the vehicle's incoming fire and escape path. Coordinate before approaching. A driver may reverse, rotate or abandon the position, and a support player behind the vehicle can be crushed or exposed. Secure the lane, repair in short windows, and stop when the threat returns.

Review the [vehicle catalogue](/en/items/vehicles) before planning support. Light transports, armor, aircraft and stationary systems create different fuel, ammunition, repair and security demands. Do not infer a final repair rate or component model from a single pre-release clip.

## Reconnaissance and observation

Observation tools are valuable when information reaches players who can act. Report target type, location, direction, movement and urgency, then update or remove stale marks. Avoid flooding the map with markers that hide the one target the squad needs.

Reconnaissance should also protect logistics. Watch approaches to the FOB, identify ambush points on cargo routes, and check where a third faction can enter. Information that prevents a full vehicle or supply loss can be worth more than one distant kill.

## Breaching and anti-vehicle equipment

Specialist explosives and launchers require clear identification, safe spacing and a follow-up plan. Confirm whether the target is infantry cover, a vehicle, a structure or an obstacle. Warn teammates before firing or placing equipment, and avoid spending the only specialist charge on a target that the squad cannot exploit.

The [weapons catalogue](/en/items/weapons) records observed launcher names and evidence status. Missing penetration, lock, blast or damage values remain unknown. Select the tool for the target class, then verify behavior in the current build rather than importing assumptions from another game.

## Attachments are tools too

An optic or magazine changes what the primary can do and what the loadout weighs or costs. Use the [attachments catalogue](/en/items/attachments) to match the expected distance. Maximum magnification is not automatically best inside buildings, while a large magazine is not automatically worth its price and inventory footprint.

Change one attachment at a time and test sight picture, obstruction, recoil recovery and reload flow. Keep a simple baseline so a new patch can be compared without confusing several changes.

## Squad equipment checklist

1. Name the task and objective window.
2. Assign one primary user and one backup who understands the tool.
3. Confirm storage, ammunition, fuel, build or medical resource as relevant.
4. Choose transport and a protected approach.
5. Set the security and communication plan.
6. Use the tool for a result, not merely because it was purchased.
7. Recover, resupply or abandon it when the objective moves.

## FAQ

**What should a beginner carry?** One tool tied to the squad plan, a complete basic weapon kit, protection, and enough storage and supplies to use the tool.

**Are prices and capacities final?** No. Tool cost, charges, capacity, repair and construction values can change with the build.

**When is a specialist tool worth a slot?** When the squad can move it to a defined target, protect the user, supply it, and exploit the result.
