## The answer that does not go stale

For **players online now**, open the [SteamDB chart for WARDOGS App 1867240](https://steamdb.info/app/1867240/charts/) and look at its current read time. A number copied into a static wiki page would become outdated within minutes. SteamDB is an independent tracker, not an official developer announcement. Technically inclined readers can also request [Valve's current-player endpoint for App 1867240](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamUserStats/GetNumberOfCurrentPlayers/v1/?appid=1867240); Valve's [Steamworks documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/ISteamUserStats) defines this as the number active in the specified Steam app at that moment.

## Numbers BULKHEAD has actually confirmed

In the official **September 12 Patch 0.11 announcement**, BULKHEAD thanked players for **400,000 peak concurrent users**. This is a historical peak milestone, not a claim that 400,000 people remain online continuously. On **September 15**, the studio announced **two million copies sold**. Sales are cumulative; they cannot be substituted for active players, daily unique users, available server slots or a current concurrent count.

An independent chart may show a more precise or higher peak than the rounded official milestone because its sampling and update time differ. Label such a number as a third-party observation with its timestamp. Do not silently present it as BULKHEAD's official figure.

## Read the right app and metric

The live product on Steam is **WARDOGS App 1867240**. The older WARDOGS Playtest was a separate beta client, so its beta-weekend activity should not be added to the main game's Early Access count. Valve's method takes an app ID: it counts people active in that particular Steam app at the time of the request, not everyone who bought it or ever played it. It also does not prove whether login queues, matchmaking or individual game servers are healthy.

When comparing two screenshots, check four things: the app ID, the UTC read time, whether the number is *playing now* or a *24-hour/all-time peak*, and whether the source is Valve, a third-party tracker or a developer announcement. A decline from a launch peak is not by itself evidence that a game is unavailable; a rising count does not prove a specific server is working.
