## Quick Answer

The best WARDOGS settings prioritize a stable frame rate and predictable frame times during 100-player combat, vehicles, destruction and base building. Match your starting preset to the **official requirements**, test a real crowded objective, then lower shadows, effects and other expensive options gradually. BULKHEAD's official performance devlog introduces **Potato Mode**, **Overkill Mode**, and a **60+ FPS** minimum development target; exact controls and results remain build- and hardware-sensitive.

## Confirmed Facts

- Steam's minimum target is 1080p Low at 60fps using upscaling on a GTX 1660 or RX 590-class GPU.
- Steam's recommended target is 1440p Medium at 70fps+ native, or 4K Medium at 60fps+ with upscaling, on an RTX 3070 or RX 6700 XT-class GPU.
- Both official tiers list 16 GB RAM and 50 GB available storage.
- WARDOGS combines a high player count with vehicles, construction and destruction, so an empty training area is not a sufficient performance test.
- BULKHEAD's official performance devlog introduces Potato Mode for lower-end PCs, Overkill Mode for powerful systems and a 60+ FPS minimum development target; those modes are separate from the Steam requirements table.

## What Players Search For

Searches for `best graphics settings wardogs`, `wardogs fps` and `how optimized is wardogs` usually ask two different questions. One is whether the PC meets the published target. The other is which settings reduce combat drops without making distant players, vehicles and objective markers difficult to read.

There is no honest universal preset. A CPU-limited machine in a 100-player fight behaves differently from a GPU-limited machine at 4K. The official devlog establishes the intended performance range, while creator footage shows one build in practice. The useful process is to create a repeatable baseline, change one group of settings, and judge the worst combat moments rather than the highest number seen while standing still.

## Baseline Test

1. Update the graphics driver and restart Windows before comparing results.
2. Close overlays or background tools that create obvious stutter, but keep normal voice chat if that reflects real play.
3. Begin with fullscreen, your monitor's native resolution and the preset closest to Steam's published target for your hardware.
4. Test inside a populated Control Zone with vehicles, smoke, explosions and construction visible.
5. Record average FPS only as context; pay more attention to repeated hitches, delayed input and the lowest sustained moments.
6. Change one setting group at a time and repeat the same kind of battle.

Do not use another player's screenshot as proof of your result. Resolution, CPU, driver, memory speed, background software and the current game build all matter.

## Competitive Priorities

Frame-time consistency comes first. A stable 60 FPS usually feels better than a higher average interrupted by large drops when a building collapses or several vehicles enter the objective. If the GPU is saturated, reduce resolution scale or upscaling quality carefully, then lower shadows, effects and post-processing before sacrificing every texture and view-distance option.

Keep visibility readable. Very low effects can reduce clutter, but an excessively soft upscaled image may hide distant movement. Texture quality may depend more on available VRAM than raw GPU speed, while shadows and effects often create a more immediate frame-rate change. Exact option names are build-sensitive, so use the live menu rather than expecting a permanent table.

Disable motion blur and film-style effects if they interfere with target tracking, when those toggles are available. Field of view can improve awareness but may make distant targets appear smaller and can alter GPU load. Choose a value you can aim with consistently rather than copying a maximum number.

## Potato Mode and Overkill Mode

BULKHEAD's official performance devlog introduces **Potato Mode** for lower-end hardware and **Overkill Mode** for systems capable of the highest visual settings. It also sets **60+ FPS** as the team's minimum performance bar during development. This confirms the modes and intent, but it is not a universal frame-rate guarantee for every scene, configuration, driver, or future build.

If Potato Mode appears in your build, treat it as a starting point and still test readability, input response and crowded fights. If Overkill Mode appears, use it for visual quality or screenshots only after proving that the system has stable headroom. Maximum settings are not automatically the best competitive settings.

## CPU, GPU and Network Diagnosis

If lowering resolution substantially improves performance, the GPU was probably a major limit. If resolution changes do little while large fights still drop, the CPU, simulation load or current build may be the constraint. Watch temperatures and clock behavior before assuming the game alone is responsible.

Network delay is not the same as render stutter. Rubber-banding, delayed hit feedback and server queues can occur while local FPS remains stable. Use a wired connection where practical and check official service posts during a test window before repeatedly reinstalling graphics files.

## FAQ

**What settings should a GTX 1660 use?**
Steam targets 1080p Low at 60fps with upscaling for the minimum GTX 1660-class configuration. Start there and test a crowded objective.

**Is Potato Mode officially guaranteed to hold 60 FPS?**
Potato Mode and the 60+ FPS development target are official, but no universal per-PC guarantee is published. Hardware, scene and build conditions still matter.

**Should an RTX 3070 use Overkill Mode?**
Not by default. Steam's recommended RTX 3070 target uses Medium settings. Test Overkill only when the live build offers it and your system retains stable headroom.

**Why is FPS good at spawn but poor in battle?**
Large fights add players, vehicles, effects, destruction and construction. Benchmark the busy Control Zone, not an empty location.
