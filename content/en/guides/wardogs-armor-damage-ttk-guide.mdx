## Quick Answer

WARDOGS survivability is a chain, not one armor number. Weapon, ammunition, distance, hit location, current health, protection coverage, movement and follow-up time all affect a fight. The complete live armor formula and a universal time-to-kill table are **not confirmed**. Any Alpha observation is build-sensitive, so use this guide to test the current build instead of memorizing permanent shot counts.

## Confirmed Facts

Official descriptions confirm realistic gunplay tuned for playability, persistent loadouts and combined-arms battles. Dated gameplay confirms multiple weapons, ammunition types and protection choices. No official source currently provides a complete live damage model, armor durability table, hit-zone multiplier set, or universal TTK chart.

## What Players Search For

The common questions are how many shots armor stops, which ammunition defeats armor, whether heavy armor is always best, and which gun kills fastest. A reliable answer must include weapon, ammunition, range, hit location, target state, protection and build version. This guide shows how to control those variables instead of presenting an unsupported number.

## How to Use This Guide

Read the variable and mission sections before comparing equipment, then use the repeatable test procedure in the current build. Record unknown conditions explicitly. Use the linked gear, ammunition and weapon records to identify what was observed and when, and discard a result after a balance change until it is retested.

## What is confirmed

Official pages describe realistic gunplay balanced for playability inside a 100-player combined-arms game with progression, cash, vehicles and logistics. Available catalogue footage shows multiple weapons, ammunition categories and equipment choices. Those sources establish that loadout decisions matter; they do not establish final damage multipliers, penetration, armor durability, limb modifiers or healing timings.

The [gear catalogue](/en/items/gear) records observed armor, helmets, packs and storage, while the [ammo catalogue](/en/items/ammo) separates visible ammunition types. Labels such as standard, flesh-damage or armor-piercing are useful clues, but a label alone does not prove the exact effect against every protection class.

## Variables that change an engagement

First control the weapon and fire mode. A burst that misses cannot be compared with a controlled semi-auto string. Second control ammunition: two compatible rounds may have different intended jobs. Third record range because recoil, visibility and any falloff can change practical time to kill. Fourth record hit location and armor coverage. A helmet result cannot be generalized to a torso plate or an uncovered limb.

Also record target state. A player may already be wounded, recently treated, moving, suppressed, or partly behind cover. Network conditions and frame timing can distort a video-only count. If any variable is unknown, mark the result as an observation rather than a confirmed damage value.

## Armor selection by mission

Protection is valuable only when the player can still perform the role. An assault player crossing exposed ground may accept greater cost and weight for coverage. A flanker may value mobility, stamina and a smaller silhouette. A medic needs enough storage and movement to reach casualties. A logistics player who avoids the front should not spend the team's entire budget to solve a risk that route planning could avoid.

Compare complete kits, not armor in isolation. Add the primary, magazines, treatment supplies and role equipment, then check price, weight and storage. If protection forces you to remove the tool that makes the role useful, the result is not automatically stronger.

## Ammunition decisions

Use standard ammunition as the baseline when the build provides it, then test specialist types under the same conditions. Flesh-focused ammunition may trade performance against protection; armor-piercing ammunition may trade price, availability or another characteristic. Those relationships must be measured in the current version. Do not assume real-world cartridge behavior maps directly to the game.

Keep different ammunition in clearly separated magazines where the interface allows. Swapping the wrong magazine invalidates a test and can lose a real fight. The [weapons catalogue](/en/items/weapons) lists observed calibre and fire modes, while the reload guide explains why loose ammunition and magazines are separate inventory decisions.

## A repeatable TTK test

1. Record the date, build label, platform and server conditions.
2. Use one weapon, optic, stance and range marker.
3. Equip a known armor item and start at a known health state.
4. Fire at one clearly defined hit zone with one ammunition type.
5. Record hits, misses, incapacitation, death and any visible armor response separately.
6. Repeat the case several times before changing one variable.
7. Publish the conditions with the result and retire it after a balance patch.

This process measures the build. It does not create an eternal tier list. Practical TTK also includes target acquisition, recoil recovery, reload interruption and whether the shooter survives long enough to land the next shot.

## Surviving longer without more armor

Use hard cover, avoid repeated peeks, cross open ground with suppression or vehicles, and stop presenting the same angle to multiple teams. Enter a fight with a loaded compatible magazine and a recovery plan. Spacing reduces the chance that one explosive or burst disables the entire squad. Communication can prevent more deaths than one expensive plate.

When wounded, break line of sight before treating. A medic should not revive in the same exposed lane that caused the casualty. Because three factions contest objectives, secure more than the obvious enemy direction before committing to a long recovery interaction.

## FAQ

**How many shots does armor absorb?** There is no confirmed universal answer because coverage, ammunition, range, hit location and build all matter.

**Is heavier armor always better?** No. Compare protection with cost, storage, mobility and the job the player must perform.

**How should damage be tested?** Hold every variable constant, repeat the case, record misses and target state, and attach the build and date.
