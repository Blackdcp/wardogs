## Quick Answer

A WARDOGS FOB is a player-built forward logistics position, not a magic respawn marker. Place it where supply vehicles can reach it, where terrain protects key structures, and where the team can influence the active objective. Feed it with supplies, add only the defenses the situation needs, and protect its transport and spawn assets.

## How do I build a FOB in WARDOGS?

Carry or purchase the current build's FOB deployment item, place it on reachable terrain with room for trucks or helicopters, and establish the smallest useful logistics loop before adding expensive defenses. Coordinate supplies and builders first; walls, trenches, mortars, anti-air and resource structures only help when the team can keep the position fed and defended.

## Why can't I spawn at a WARDOGS FOB?

A FOB is not automatically a spawn point. Observed builds use a separately deployed team spawn vehicle and require the correct score, deployment state or cooldown before players can appear there. Protect that vehicle and confirm the current interface instead of assuming the FOB core itself provides spawning.

## Confirmed Facts

- Building, destruction, logistics, vehicles, and team support are core WARDOGS systems.
- FOBs require players to move resources and maintain the position rather than replenishing everything automatically.
- Observed test builds include fortifications, trenches, anti-air, mortars, resource structures, and separate vehicle-based spawn support.
- Construction tools and structures can be gated by equipment or progression.
- Exact costs, capacities, placement rules, and unlock levels can change between Closed Beta and Early Access.

## What Players Search For

Players searching `wardogs fob guide` usually want to know why they cannot spawn on a new base, what supplies actually do, where oil rigs and mortars fit, and why an expensive position disappears so quickly. The answer is that a FOB is a network of dependent assets. The foundation, supplies, builders, delivery route, defenses, and spawn transport all need to work together.

A strong base reduces travel, supports the Control Zone, and gives vehicles or infantry a place to recover. A bad one drains cash and manpower in a location the team cannot supply or defend.

## How to Use This Guide

Use **Placement** before deploying the first structure, **Build Order** while supplies are limited, **Supply and Spawn Flow** to keep the position useful, and **Attacking a FOB** when the opposing team has established one near the objective.

## Placement

Choose terrain with more than visual concealment. The site needs a practical road or landing approach, enough flat space for delivery and maneuver, cover from direct fire, and a route toward the current or likely objective. Avoid sealing the only vehicle entrance with walls, placing the core on a skyline, or building so tightly that helicopters and trucks cannot unload.

Think about artillery and air observation as well as infantry. Terrain folds, reverse slopes, tree lines, and hard cover can reduce exposure, but a base too far behind the fight may never repay the supply effort. A base directly inside the hottest line may be discovered before it has defenses or a functioning logistics loop.

## Build Order

Start with the required FOB foundation or deployment item, then establish the minimum services the squad needs. Secure access, create protected storage or supply handling, and coordinate the separate spawn vehicle if the build uses one. Walls and trenches should shape approaches rather than form an attractive but unusable enclosure.

Add specialist systems for a reason. Anti-air is valuable when aircraft threaten the site. Mortars can support a known target and spotter. Resource structures can improve the economic value of the position. Building all of them immediately can exhaust supplies and advertise the base before the team can defend it.

## Resource Types, Hammers and Unloading

Closed Beta footage separates FOB supply into **ammo, build, fuel and mechanical** resources. Check the destination request before loading a truck or aircraft: ammunition supports fighting positions, build resources feed construction, fuel keeps vehicles operating, and mechanical parts support selected vehicles or specialist systems. The live interface remains authoritative if names or recipes change.

Construction footage also shows **small, medium and large** hammers tied to different structure tiers. Do not assume one cheap hammer can place or finish every asset. Leave inventory space for the correct tool, bring supply pallets into the FOB area, and make sure a teammate can unload them instead of dropping cargo where it cannot be converted into usable stock.

Protect the FOB core while builders work. A hacked or compromised core can threaten the entire investment, so assign observation and a response route before adding decorative walls or another specialist emplacement.

## Supply and Spawn Flow

FOBs depend on repeat logistics. Assign drivers or pilots, agree on a route, and keep the unloading area clear. Escort valuable deliveries through predictable choke points and vary the approach when the enemy starts watching it. Supply players need communication from builders so they know whether the next run should sustain ammunition, construction, defenses, or another asset.

Observed builds use a mobile spawn vehicle that must be deployed and protected; do not tell the squad to click the FOB icon and expect a direct spawn without checking the current interface. Park spawn support where it is useful but not visible from the first enemy approach, and move it when the front changes.

## Defense

Use layered defense: observation outside the walls, infantry cover near likely breaches, anti-vehicle tools covering roads, and a reserve able to repair or counterattack. Static weapons without spotters are easy to bypass. A perimeter with no supply team eventually becomes an empty shell.

The active Control Zone can move, so decide when to hold, reduce, or abandon a position. Defending a beautiful base that no longer influences scoring can cost the match. Preserve mobile assets and players when the strategic value disappears.

## Attacking a FOB

Find the logistics route before charging the center. Interdict trucks, aircraft, or exposed resource activity; then identify the spawn vehicle, defensive weapons, and core structures. Use smoke, suppression, explosives, vehicles, or construction-destruction tools according to the current build and squad role. A coordinated attack that removes supply and spawn capability is more reliable than repeated isolated assaults.

Do not publish one “correct” demolition price or health value as permanent. Closed Beta numbers can change. The durable method is reconnaissance, isolation, suppression, breach, and destruction of the assets that keep the position operational.

## Purchase, Carry, and Place the FOB Item

Closed Beta players described the initial FOB as a vendor item that occupied a 2x3 inventory area. After purchase, the carrier could place it on a quick slot from the inventory opened with Tab, select that slot, choose valid ground and hold the placement input. Once active, the FOB created an influence area for additional construction and team activity.

Every detail in that sequence is build-sensitive: price, inventory size, hotkey, placement hold time, terrain validation and influence radius can change. The useful preparation is to leave enough 2x3 space, confirm the live quick-slot prompt and choose a site with cover, supply access, room for vehicles and a defensible exit before buying.

## Pallets, Crates, and Resource Types

A FOB is not sustained by the radio or base item alone. Supply pallets and crates feed construction, ammunition and other functions. Before a transport run, identify what the destination actually lacks and where the load can be unloaded safely. Delivering the wrong resource or blocking the landing area creates activity without creating value.

Separate the jobs: one player requests the resource, one transports it, one unloads or moves it into the usable area, and the builders spend it on the current priority. Recheck live icons and labels because Closed Beta terminology and capacities may change.

## Tower FOB or Remote Fire-Support FOB?

A tower-side FOB shortens the reinforcement route to an objective but is easier for enemies to discover and pressure. A remote fire-support FOB can protect mortars and supplies, but it needs reconnaissance, transport and a reliable link to the main fight. Choose the base type before placing it; trying to serve every purpose from one exposed position usually creates a costly target.

## Cargo Controls and the FOB Delivery Loop

The August 28 cargo walkthrough makes the supply loop more concrete. In that recorded build, **B** opened the vehicle logistics screen, **G** dropped the selected load, and **F** unloaded it when the truck was correctly positioned. Smaller and larger crates served lighter deliveries, while the **Ural** handled heavy pallets. Treat those keys and capacities as beta observations, not permanent bindings.

The footage distinguishes building, ammunition, fuel, and mechanical pallets. A builder should request the exact resource before the truck leaves main base. The driver confirms the cargo in the logistics screen, announces the route, approaches the FOB slowly, leaves the spawn and road clear, unloads, and verifies that the base accepted the pallet. Yellow construction marks and valid placement previews help builders identify where a structure can snap into place.

This creates a cleaner division of labor: requester, driver, escort, unloader, and builder. If an Oil Rig needs fuel or a FOB needs building supplies, do not substitute a visually similar pallet. Use the dedicated [cargo guide](/guides/wardogs-cargo-guide) and [Oil Rig guide](/guides/wardogs-oil-rig-guide) for the current workflow.

## FAQ

**Is a FOB the same as a spawn point?**
No. It is a forward operating and logistics position; observed builds use separate spawn-vehicle deployment.

**What should be built first?**
Build the foundation and the smallest useful logistics/spawn flow before expensive specialist defenses.

**Why did our FOB run out of supplies?**
FOBs do not sustain themselves. Construction, ammunition, repairs, and defenses depend on deliveries and coordinated use.

**Are mortar and anti-air values final?**
No. Availability, cost, damage, range, and unlock rules seen in tests should be labeled by build and rechecked after updates.
