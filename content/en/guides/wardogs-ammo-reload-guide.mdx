## Quick Answer

A WARDOGS weapon, magazine, and ammunition are separate items. At the vendor, choose the gun, select a compatible magazine, select the correct ammunition, and add the prepared magazine to the weapon. Equip a backpack before adding spare magazines. During a fight, **R swaps magazines; it does not refill them**. Open inventory behind cover and use compatible **loose ammo** to refill partial magazines. The Closed Beta displayed **C** as the Combine Ammo shortcut, but verify the live binding.

## The Three Pieces You Must Match

Every magazine-fed loadout needs a weapon, a magazine made for that weapon or caliber, and rounds compatible with that magazine. Owning eighty loose rounds does not place them in the gun. Owning several empty magazines does not give them ammunition. The vendor workflow combines those pieces into a ready magazine.

Read caliber and compatibility instead of buying by appearance. A cartridge with a similar name can still be wrong. Until the live catalogue is fully verified, use the vendor's compatible options and item tooltips rather than an old beta price table.

## Buying the First Loaded Magazine

Select the weapon, open its magazine or ammunition area, choose the magazine, then choose the ammunition type. The resulting ready magazine must be added to the loadout. The first prepared magazine normally occupies the weapon. Additional clicks or purchases create spare magazines only when the player has storage.

If the game refuses a spare, equip a pouch or backpack first and check its grid. Inventory shape matters as well as total capacity. Do not buy a large bag only for ammunition; medical supplies and role tools compete for the same space.

## What R Actually Does

Pressing **R** removes the current magazine and inserts another carried magazine. The old magazine returns to inventory with its remaining rounds. A half-used magazine remains half used, and an empty one remains empty. After several early reloads, the bag can contain multiple partial magazines that later cycle back into the gun.

That behavior explains the common “my reload only gave me a few bullets” problem. The game did not lose the ammunition; the player selected another partial magazine. Consolidate rounds after the fight instead of repeatedly reloading in panic.

## Loose Ammo and Repacking

Carry at least one sensible stack of loose ammunition for the weapon's caliber. Behind hard cover, open inventory, identify a partial compatible magazine, and use the loose stack on it. Community reports and the recorded interface describe drag-and-drop loading and a **Combine Ammo** control. In the August test, **C** combined compatible loose rounds into carried magazines.

Do not repack while exposed. The inventory blocks awareness and the loading action takes time. Move into a building, behind terrain, or into a protected vehicle or FOB area. Tell the squad if the player must stop for ammunition instead of silently falling behind.

## Spare Magazines vs Loose Rounds

Ready magazines provide immediate reloads; loose rounds provide compact endurance. Too few magazines create long gaps under pressure. Too many consume storage and cash that could carry bandages, armor, explosives, or a role tool. A conservative beginner setup is one magazine in the weapon, one or two spares, and one compatible loose stack, then adjust after learning the fight length.

This is a method, not a permanent quantity. Magazine size, stack size, weight, price, and reload behavior can change with the build. Check the current item data and carry only what the role can realistically use before death or resupply.

## Ammo Types

Recorded builds separated standard, flesh-focused, and armor-focused ammunition. Do not mix types without tracking which magazine contains which rounds. A compatible caliber can load while producing a different damage profile. New players should usually keep one type per weapon until they can identify armor and understand the live balance.

## Fast Troubleshooting

**The gun starts empty:** confirm a prepared magazine was added to the weapon, not only placed in the backpack.

**R does nothing:** confirm a spare compatible magazine exists and has rounds.

**Loose rounds will not load:** confirm caliber compatibility, move the magazine into inventory if required, and use the current Combine Ammo prompt.

**A reload gives very few rounds:** the next magazine was probably partial. Repack carried magazines behind cover.

**The vendor blocks another magazine:** equip storage and leave enough contiguous inventory space.

## Confirmed Facts

- Weapons, compatible magazines, and loose ammunition are separate parts of the observed loadout flow.
- Reloading the weapon and filling a magazine are different actions.
- Community beta reports describe combining compatible partial stacks in the inventory.
- Input prompts, stack size, prices, and fill time can change.

## What Players Search For

Players arrive after buying ammunition that the weapon will not fire, finding several partial magazines, or seeing no refill action. The durable answer is to verify the weapon, caliber, magazine, loose ammunition, inventory space, and current interaction in that order.

## How to Use This Guide

Test one weapon-magazine-ammunition set before buying a reserve. Use the loading steps in a safe area, the stack section after combat, and the diagnostic section whenever the action is missing.

## FAQ

**Is loose ammunition a loaded magazine?** No. It is the supply used to fill a compatible magazine.

**Can partial ammunition stacks be combined?** Beta players report that compatible stacks can be combined; verify the live action and limit.

**Why should one slot remain free?** The game needs somewhere to place an empty or replaced magazine.
